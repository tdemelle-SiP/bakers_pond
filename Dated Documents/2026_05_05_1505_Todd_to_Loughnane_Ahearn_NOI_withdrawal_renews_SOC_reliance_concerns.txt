Hi Nora,

I am writing to supplement the materials I previously sent regarding 42 Mill Street.

Thank you again for confirming that neither a Conservation Commission Order of Conditions nor a MassDEP Superseding Order of Conditions can supplant the Building Commissioner's determination of whether the parcel constitutes a buildable lot.

On April 28, the applicant withdrew the dam repair/removal Notice of Intent (DEP File #338-0756), which the Westwood Conservation Commission had denied. That NOI concerned dam-related work required under the settlement agreement through which the Commission accepted the house SOC. The withdrawal letter states that withdrawal leaves the property subject to the May 6, 2022 SOC for construction of a single-family house, associated infrastructure, utilities, and grading. In other words, the applicant appears to be treating withdrawal of the dam-related filing as leaving no remaining obstacle to proceeding under the house SOC.

I have asked MassDEP to address whether unresolved wetlands issues affect continued reliance on SOC #338-0706 for house construction.

Separately, I want to renew the Planning/Building concern already raised: the materials submitted for 42 Mill Street appear to rely on unresolved or erroneous lot-line and access assumptions. I previously sent the lot-line excerpt to Susanne Hogan, and the Conservation Commission also addressed that issue in its denial of the dam repair/removal NOI.

I ask that no building permit or related municipal approval issue for 42 Mill Street until the Building Department, Planning Division, Town Counsel, and Conservation Commission have coordinated on whether SOC #338-0706 can be relied upon for house construction given these unresolved issues.
