Dear Ms. Loughnane and Mr. Ahearn,
I am writing to supplement the Planning, Building, Conservation Commission, and Town Counsel records for 42 Mill Street.
On May 19, in response to a records request, I received the administrative record for WET-2016-022 from MassDEP's Office of Appeals and Dispute Resolution (attached). This OADR record contains the first direct explanation I have found for why the SORAD's run-of-river finding was not carried into the FORAD.
I understand that the 2022 SOC #338-0706 authorizing house construction at 42 Mill Street remains in effect and with the withdrawal of the dam NOI, the Town cannot reopen MassDEP's wetlands determinations. I raise these findings with you because the newly obtained OADR record shows that the 2016 FORAD's reversal of MassDEP's run-of-river finding in the SORAD rests on a thin settlement explanation that does not engage the underlying analysis. That creates risk if the SOC is treated as a clean basis for routine local approvals on the lot as it exists today.
In MassDEP's August 25, 2016 SORAD, Deputy Regional Director Rachel Freed made two distinct findings:
1. Mill Brook is a perennial stream, based on watershed size, USGS topographic mapping, predicted flow rate, Public Water Supply recharge area, and observations made during an abnormally dry period.
2. The Mill Brook impoundment itself has primarily riverine characteristics under 310 CMR 10.58 and is therefore run-of-

river. Freed based this on the impoundment's extremely shallow depth, its size relative to the watershed, the short inlet-tooutlet distance, and the 1941 USGS quadrangle showing a channel through wetland with no impoundment at the site.

The applicant's September 8, 2016 Notice of Claim challenged Freed's classification by asserting that "the on-site pond is identified as a pond on the most recent USGS map and also lacks riverine characteristics." No specifics supporting either statement are provided. Furthermore, I have found no USGS map that identifies the impoundment as a pond as the applicant claims (which is not required by the run-of-river regulation in any event).

The matter moved quickly into settlement after the appeal. MassDEP's Motion to Approve Settlement states that the FORAD showed the impounded BVW "as originally proposed by the Applicant" without the run-of-river classification because "discussions with Applicant's wetlands consultant and further review of the evidence indicated that the perennial stream did not flow through this area."

That sentence, the only explanation I have found in any record for the DEP's reversal, simply claims the stream does not flow through the area. It does not address either of Freed's technically grounded findings in the SORAD. Neither Freed's perennial determination nor her run-of-river conclusion turn on the stream flowing through in any particular way. They turn on a detailed analysis of the impoundment and its watershed under the regulations. The Motion's justification does not address the SORAD's findings, much less provide a basis for overturning them.

Before any related municipal approval proceeds, I ask that the Town consider the applicant's reliance on the now tenyear-old FORAD and the 2022 SOC #338-0706 in light of current site conditions and these unresolved record, access, lotline, and settlement-related issues.
